Hiring is slow when it's done in between everything else. A staffing company in India takes the sourcing, screening and coordination off your team's plate so that hiring managers only spend time on candidates who are worth meeting. This guide explains how that works in practice, how to choose between contract staffing and permanent hiring, and what to check before you work with any staffing partner — including us.

## What a staffing company actually does

The word "staffing" covers a few different services, and it helps to be clear about which one you need.

- **Permanent recruitment.** The staffing partner finds, screens and presents candidates. You make the offer and the person joins your payroll. The partner's fee is usually tied to a successful hire.
- **Contract staffing.** The professional works for you, but is employed by the staffing company, which handles payroll, statutory compliance and contract paperwork for the agreed period.
- **Contract-to-hire.** The person starts on contract and can move to your payroll after a trial period if both sides want that.
- **Bulk or project hiring.** Many similar roles filled to a deadline — for example, a support team for a new process or engineers for a fixed-scope project.
- **Executive search.** Senior and leadership roles, where the work is more about discreet outreach and assessment than about volume.

A good partner also does the unglamorous work in between: writing a job description candidates actually understand, checking notice periods and salary expectations before an interview is booked, chasing feedback, and keeping candidates warm while you decide.

## Contract staffing vs permanent hiring

Neither model is better in general. The right choice depends on how long you need the skill, how certain the role is, and how much administration you want to own.

| Question | Contract staffing | Permanent hiring |
|---|---|---|
| How long is the need? | A project, a peak or an uncertain period | Ongoing, core to the business |
| How fast must someone start? | Often faster — contractors tend to be available sooner | Depends on the candidate's notice period |
| Who handles payroll and compliance? | The staffing company | You |
| Long-term knowledge retention | Lower, unless you convert the person | Higher |
| Flexibility to scale down | High, within the contract terms | Low |

### When contract staffing makes sense

Choose contract staffing when the work has a clear end date or when you are not yet sure the role will last. Typical cases include a migration or implementation project, a short-term backlog, cover for someone on long leave, or a new process you want to test before committing headcount. In IT staffing in particular, specialist skills — a specific cloud platform, a niche ERP module, a testing framework — are often needed for a few months rather than for years.

### When permanent hiring is the better call

Hire permanently when the role is core to how you make money, when you want someone to grow into a bigger position, or when the person will hold knowledge you can't afford to lose. Permanent hires also tend to fit better into team culture over time because they plan their careers around you.

### Contract-to-hire: a middle path

If you are unsure, contract-to-hire lets both sides find out whether the fit is right before committing. Be honest with candidates about the conversion criteria and timeline. Vague promises of "we'll see" lead to good people leaving early.

## IT staffing: what's different

Technology roles move quickly and candidates usually hold multiple offers. A few things matter more in IT staffing than elsewhere:

1. **Precise skill matching.** "Java developer" can mean very different things. A good recruiter asks which frameworks, which kind of systems, and what the person will actually build in the first three months.
2. **Technical screening before your interview.** Even a short structured screening call saves your engineers hours.
3. **Speed of feedback.** Strong candidates accept other offers while a company deliberates. Agree on interview slots and feedback turnaround up front.
4. **Notice periods and buyouts.** Many experienced candidates in India serve long notice periods. Ask early, and decide whether you can wait or support an early release.

## How to choose a staffing partner

Before you sign with any staffing company in India, ask these questions:

- **Who will actually work on my roles?** You want a named recruiter who understands your domain, not a generic inbox.
- **How do you screen?** Ask what happens between "CV found" and "CV sent to us".
- **Do you charge candidates anything?** The answer should be no. Charging job seekers is a red flag and, for recruitment of Indians for overseas jobs, it's tightly regulated.
- **How do you protect candidate data?** Profiles should only be shared with consent and only for roles the candidate agreed to.
- **What happens if a hire leaves early?** Understand the replacement terms before you need them.
- **What will reporting look like?** You should be able to see where every role stands without chasing.

## Getting started

If you are deciding between contract staffing and permanent hiring for a specific role, write down three things: how long you need the skill, how soon you need someone, and what it costs you every week the seat stays empty.
