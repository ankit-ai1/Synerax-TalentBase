Many people search for jobs in USA for Indian professionals, and the internet is full of promises that make it sound simple. The honest picture is that working in the United States requires the legal right to work there, and for most Indian citizens that means an employer-sponsored visa or a study route. This guide explains the main options in plain language, how remote work for US companies from India fits in, and how to avoid the scams that target people with this goal.

*This is general information, not immigration advice. US immigration rules change frequently — always confirm details on official US government websites or with a qualified immigration attorney.*

## The key point: work authorisation

US employers must verify that every employee is authorised to work in the United States. Unless you already hold US citizenship, a green card or another status that allows work, a US employer would need to sponsor you through an appropriate visa category. That is why most job listings in the US either state that sponsorship is available or say that they can only consider candidates already authorised to work.

When you search, look specifically for roles that mention visa sponsorship — and read the requirements carefully.

## Employer-sponsored routes

### H-1B (specialty occupations)

The H-1B category is for roles that require specialised knowledge and typically at least a bachelor's degree or equivalent in a related field. The employer files the petition, the number of new H-1B visas each year is limited, and selection for many employers happens through a registration and selection process. Details, eligibility and current procedures are on the official [USCIS H-1B page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations).

Practical implications:

- The **employer drives the process** — you can't apply for an H-1B on your own.
- Because of the annual limit and selection process, **there is no guarantee** even with a willing employer.
- Be extremely cautious of anyone asking you to pay for "H-1B sponsorship". The employer is responsible for the petition, and US rules require employers to pay certain H-1B fees themselves.

### L-1 (intra-company transfers)

If you work for a multinational company that has offices in the US, an intra-company transfer may be possible. For the L-1A category (executives and managers), the employee generally needs to have worked for the company abroad for at least one continuous year within the three years before the petition, according to [USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/l-1a-intracompany-transferee-executive-or-manager). A separate L-1B category covers specialised-knowledge employees.

For many Indian professionals, joining a company with a strong US presence and performing well is a more reliable path to working in the US than applying directly from India.

### Other categories

There are other visa categories for people with extraordinary ability, specific nationalities under trade agreements, and more. Most don't apply to the typical Indian job seeker, but an immigration attorney can advise on your specific profile.

## The study route

Many Indians first go to the US as students on an F-1 visa. After completing a degree, eligible students may be able to work for a period through Optional Practical Training (OPT), with additional time available for certain STEM degrees. See the official [USCIS page on OPT](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-opt-for-f-1-students) for eligibility and rules.

This route is a significant financial and personal commitment. Choose a programme for its educational value and your career, not only as a way to get a work visa.

## Remote work for US companies — from India

You don't have to move to work with US companies. Many hire people in India:

- **Through their Indian entity or a global capability centre,** where you're an employee in India.
- **Through an employer of record or staffing company** that employs you in India on the client's behalf.
- **As an independent contractor,** invoicing the US company directly.

Each has different implications for contracts, benefits and taxes. As a contractor you're responsible for your own taxes in India and you may need to consider GST — speak to a chartered accountant before you sign. You can browse [remote jobs in India](/jobs/remote-india) and read our guide to [finding genuine remote jobs](/blog/remote-jobs-in-india).

Time zones are the main practical challenge: most US-facing roles need several hours of overlap with US working hours, which often means evening or night work in India.

## Red flags: US job and visa scams

People who want to work in the US are frequent targets of fraud. Walk away if:

- someone **guarantees** a US visa or job;
- you're asked to **pay for sponsorship, a visa "slot" or a job offer;**
- the "employer" interviews you only over chat and sends an offer immediately;
- emails come from free email addresses or domains that imitate real companies;
- you're asked to send money to an individual's account or in cryptocurrency.

Check the employer independently and use official US government websites (ending in **.gov**) for visa information.

## Preparing your profile for US employers

If you're targeting US-based teams — whether for a move or a remote role — present your experience the way US hiring managers expect:

- **Keep your résumé to one or two pages** with a focus on outcomes rather than duties.
- **Leave out personal details** such as date of birth, marital status and photographs, which US employers generally don't expect.
- **Use clear, specific language** about your role in each project.
- **Make your LinkedIn profile match your résumé** and show recent work.

## A realistic plan

1. **Build strong, in-demand skills** and a track record in India.
2. **Target employers with US operations** — multinationals, product companies, GCCs.
3. **Consider remote roles with US teams** to gain experience and references.
4. **Get proper advice** before committing money to any study or visa route.
