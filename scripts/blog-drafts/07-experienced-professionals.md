Finding jobs in India for experienced professionals is a different exercise from a first or second job search. There are fewer roles at each level, hiring decisions involve more people, and the things that got you noticed early — degrees, certifications, enthusiasm — matter less than a clear record of results. The good news is that experienced candidates who present themselves well are in demand. Here's how to approach your next move.

## Start with what you want, not what's available

Before you update your CV, answer three questions honestly:

1. **What problem do you want to solve next?** Scale a team, build a new function, turn something around, go deeper as a specialist?
2. **What are your non-negotiables?** Location, work mode, travel, compensation floor, industry.
3. **What are you willing to trade?** Title for scope, salary for learning, or the reverse.

Experienced professionals who skip this step tend to chase every opening and end up in a role that looks good on paper but doesn't fit.

## Position yourself clearly

At senior levels, recruiters and hiring managers look for a clear story: "this person has done X, at Y scale, in Z kind of environment". Make that story easy to see.

### Write a summary that does real work

Three or four lines at the top of your CV that say what you do, where you've done it, and what you're known for. Avoid generic phrases like "result-oriented leader" — show it instead.

### Show impact, scope and context

For each recent role, include the size of your team or budget, what you inherited, what you changed, and the result. Use figures only if they're accurate and you can explain them; vague claims hurt more than they help.

### Trim the early years

You don't need every detail of your first jobs. Keep the last several years detailed and summarise the rest.

### Keep every version consistent

Your CV, LinkedIn profile and what you say in interviews must match. Senior offers almost always involve background verification, and mismatched dates or titles are a common reason offers fall through.

## Use your network — properly

Many senior roles are filled through referrals and direct outreach rather than public job ads. Reconnect with former managers, colleagues and clients before you need them. Be specific about what you're looking for so people can actually help.

## Notice periods, buyouts and timing

Long notice periods are common for experienced professionals in India, and they affect which roles you can realistically go for.

- **Know your exact notice period** and whether your employer allows early release or a buyout.
- **Be upfront** with recruiters from the first call. Hiding a long notice period only causes problems later.
- **Plan handovers** so you leave on good terms. References and reputations follow you in senior roles.

Some employers can wait; others need someone sooner and may help with a buyout. Ask rather than assume.

## Handling counter-offers

When you resign, your current employer may make a counter-offer. Before you decide, go back to why you started looking. If the reasons were about growth, leadership, culture or direction, more money rarely fixes them. If the reasons really were only compensation, a counter-offer may be worth considering — but understand that the relationship may change once you've resigned.

Whatever you decide, decide quickly and communicate clearly with everyone involved.

## Interviews at senior levels

Expect more conversations with more stakeholders: the hiring manager, peers, leadership, sometimes the board or global teams. Prepare to discuss:

- a few situations where you made a difficult decision and what happened;
- how you build and develop teams;
- what you'd focus on in your first months;
- how you've handled failure.

It's also your chance to assess them. Ask about the company's challenges, how success in the role will be measured, and why the position is open.

## Background verification and references

For senior roles, expect thorough checks: previous employment dates and titles, education, sometimes address and criminal record checks, and references from former managers. Prepare in advance:

- **Collect your documents** — relieving letters, experience letters, payslips and certificates.
- **Line up references** who have managed you directly and can speak to specific results. Ask their permission first and tell them about the role.
- **Address any gaps honestly.** A career break, a short stint or a layoff is easy to explain if you're upfront about it.

## Working with recruiters effectively

A good recruiter can open doors, but the relationship works best when it's honest in both directions.

1. **Share your real priorities** — role, location, compensation range and timing.
2. **Tell them where else you're interviewing,** at least in general terms, so they can move at the right speed.
3. **Give feedback after interviews.** It helps the recruiter position you and manage the employer's expectations.

Experience transfers more than people think — leadership, stakeholder management, process design and domain-neutral technical skills all travel well.
