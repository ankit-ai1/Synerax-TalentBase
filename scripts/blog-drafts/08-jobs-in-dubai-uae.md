The UAE — and Dubai in particular — has long been a popular destination for Indian professionals. Jobs in Dubai span construction and engineering, hospitality, aviation, healthcare, retail, finance, logistics and technology. Moving abroad for work is a big decision, though, and the process has steps that differ from hiring in India. This guide explains how it usually works and what to check, with links to official sources so you can confirm the details for your situation.

*This article is general guidance, not legal or immigration advice. Rules change; always confirm with official sources and your employer.*

## How hiring in the UAE usually works

In the UAE, a work visa for a private-sector job is generally sponsored by the employer. The typical sequence is:

1. **Interview and offer.** Many first rounds happen online; some employers interview in India or fly candidates in.
2. **Offer letter and contract.** For private-sector jobs, employment contracts are governed by the UAE's labour framework, overseen by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation ([MOHRE](https://www.mohre.gov.ae)).
3. **Entry permit and visa processing,** handled by the employer.
4. **Arrival, medical fitness test and Emirates ID.**
5. **Residence visa** stamping or issuance, after which you can open a bank account, rent a home in your name and so on.

The UAE government portal has an overview of [working in the UAE](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs) and the steps involved.

## What to check in a Dubai job offer

Read the offer carefully and ask questions before you resign from your current job:

- **Job title and duties** — do they match what was discussed?
- **Salary breakdown** — basic salary versus allowances (housing, transport and others). Some entitlements are calculated on basic salary, so the split matters.
- **Contract type and duration,** probation period and notice period.
- **Working hours and weekly off days.**
- **Housing** — provided, or an allowance?
- **Health insurance** — ask what's covered. Employers in Dubai and Abu Dhabi are required to provide health insurance for employees; see the official overview of [health insurance in the UAE](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/health-and-fitness/health-insurance).
- **Annual leave and air tickets** home.
- **End-of-service benefits** — UAE labour law provides for end-of-service gratuity; check how it applies to your contract on the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/employment-in-the-private-sector).

Get everything important in writing. If something you were promised isn't in the contract, ask for it to be added.

## Documents Indian professionals usually need

Requirements depend on the role and employer, but commonly include:

- a **valid passport** with enough validity remaining;
- **educational certificates,** which often need attestation — in India by the relevant state authority and the Ministry of External Affairs, and then by the UAE embassy or its authorised centres. Your employer will tell you what they need;
- **experience letters** from previous employers;
- **passport-size photographs** to the specified format.

### Emigration clearance (ECR passports)

If your passport is marked "Emigration Check Required" (ECR), you need emigration clearance from the Government of India before travelling for employment to certain countries, including the UAE. This is handled through the [eMigrate portal](https://emigrate.gov.in), which also lists registered recruiting agents. Holders of ECNR passports generally don't need this clearance, but check the portal for the current rules.

## Avoid recruitment fraud

Overseas job fraud is a real risk. Protect yourself:

- **Use registered recruiting agents** for overseas jobs. India's eMigrate portal lets you check whether an agent is registered.
- **Be wary of large upfront payments** for "visa", "processing" or "guarantee" fees, and of offers made without a proper interview.
- **Verify the employer** — official website, a company email domain and a real address.
- **Never travel on a visit visa for a job promised later.** Working on a visit visa is not permitted and can leave you without protection.
- **Keep copies** of your passport, contract and visa, and don't hand over your original passport to anyone except as required for official processing.

If you need help once you are in the UAE, the [Consulate General of India in Dubai](https://www.cgidubai.gov.in) and the [Embassy of India in Abu Dhabi](https://www.indembassyuae.gov.in) provide assistance to Indian nationals.

## Life in Dubai: practical points

- **Tax.** The UAE does not levy personal income tax on salaries; check the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/finance-and-investment/taxation) for current details. Your tax position in India depends on your residential status — speak to a chartered accountant.
- **Cost of living.** Housing is usually the biggest expense. Factor in rent, school fees if you have children, transport and utilities when you compare an offer with your current salary.
- **Culture and law.** The UAE is multicultural, but local laws and customs apply — read up before you move.

## Preparing for interviews with UAE employers

Interviews for Gulf roles are often conducted online in the early stages, with candidates in different countries. A few tips:

- **Be clear about your availability** — your notice period, when you could travel and whether you have any documents still to arrange.
- **Understand the role's context.** Many UAE employers serve multinational clients and work with colleagues from many countries. Highlight experience working in diverse teams.
- **Prepare questions about the package,** but ask them at the right stage — usually once the employer has shown clear interest.
- **Keep your documents ready** to share digitally when asked: passport copy, certificates and experience letters.

## Before you resign from your current job

Don't hand in your notice until you have a written offer, a signed contract you've read carefully, and clarity on visa processing timelines. Delays happen — a medical test, attestation or approval can take longer than expected — so keep a buffer and stay in touch with your employer throughout.
